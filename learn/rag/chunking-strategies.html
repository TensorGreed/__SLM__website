    Chunking strategies for RAG

    Chunking cuts your documents into the pieces a retriever indexes and returns, so it decides whether the passage that answers a question sits whole inside one retrievable piece. For a small embedding model with a 256-token input, a sensible starting point is recursive splitting (page or paragraph breaks first, then sentences, then words) at a few hundred characters, checked with recall@k on questions about your own documents. On 30 questions about IRS Publication 17, with about 1,600 to 1,800 characters retrieved, 256-character chunks contained the answer passage for 25 questions and 1,024-character chunks for 18, a gap that 30 questions can suggest but not prove (p = 0.065).

    Runs on: CPU, about three minutes for every block on the page once the model is downloaded Updated: 26 Nov 2026

    Tested on 26 Nov 2026 · Python 3.12.3 · sentence-transformers 3.3.1 · transformers 4.46.3 · torch 2.5.1 · rank-bm25 0.2.2 · pypdf 5.1.0 · numpy 2.1.3 · CPU (20-core Arm, no GPU)

    What chunking decides

    A retriever never sees your document, only its chunks: it embeds each one, compares them with the question and hands the closest few to the model. Three things depend on where the cuts fall.

    • Whether the answer is whole. A sentence split across two chunks may match neither, and the chunk that comes back may hold half the fact.
    • What one vector stands for. A long chunk about three topics gets a vector a little like all three and close to none. Models also have limits: all-MiniLM-L6-v2, used below, reads 256 word pieces, drops the rest, and was trained on inputs of at most 128.
    • How much the model reads. Five chunks of 1,000 characters is four times the context of five chunks of 250.

    Fixed-size

    sample = (
    "If you are a U.S. citizen or resident, whether you must file a return depends on "
    "three factors: your gross income, your filing status, and your age.\n\n"
    "Limit on amount of refund. If you file your claim within 3 years after the date you "
    "filed your return, the credit or refund can't be more than the part of the tax paid "
    "within the 3-year period immediately before you filed the claim.\n\n"
    "Tax paid. Payments, including estimated tax payments, made before the due date of the "
    "original return are considered paid on the due date. For example, income tax withheld "
    "during the year is considered paid on the due date of the return, which is April 15 "
    "for most taxpayers."
)

def fixed_size(text, size, overlap=0):
    step = size - overlap
    return [text[i:i + size] for i in range(0, max(len(text) - overlap, 1), step)]

def show(chunks):
    for i, c in enumerate(chunks):
        print(f"{i}  {len(c):>3} chars  {c[:22]!r} ... {c[-22:]!r}")

print(len(sample), "characters")
show(fixed_size(sample, 200))

    Sentence-based

    import re

def sentences(text):
    # a sentence ends at . ! or ? plus whitespace; the whitespace stays with it
    return re.findall(r".+?(?:[.!?]\s+|$)", text, flags=re.S)

def pack(pieces, size):
    chunks, buf = [], ""
    for p in pieces:
        if buf and len(buf) + len(p) > size:
            chunks.append(buf)
            buf = ""
        buf += p
    if buf:
        chunks.append(buf)
    return chunks

print([s[:24] for s in sentences(sample)[:3]])
show(pack(sentences(sample), 200))

    Output

    ['If you are a U.S. ', 'citizen or resident, whe', 'Limit on amount of refun']

2  139 chars  'Tax paid. Payments, in' ... 'paid on the due date. '

    The first line shows the classic failure: the regex ended a sentence at "U.S.". Both halves landed in chunk 0 here; at a smaller size they could be split apart. Chunk 1 is 207 characters, over the limit, because pack never cuts a sentence.

    Paragraph-based

    def paragraphs(text):
    return [p for p in re.split(r"(?<=\n\n)", text) if p]

    Recursive splitting

    def split_keep(text, sep):
    # split on sep but keep it, so the pieces join back to the exact text
    if sep == "":
        return list(text)
    parts = text.split(sep)
    return [p + sep for p in parts[:-1]] + ([parts[-1]] if parts[-1] else [])

def recursive_split(text, size, seps=("\n\n", "\n", ". ", " ", "")):
    if len(text) <= size:
        return [text]
    pieces = []
    for piece in split_keep(text, seps[0]):
        if len(piece) <= size:
            pieces.append(piece)
        else:                      # too big: split it again with the next separator
            pieces += recursive_split(piece, size, seps[1:])
    return pack(pieces, size)      # every piece fits, so no chunk is over size

chunks = recursive_split(sample, 200)
show(chunks)
print("lossless:", "".join(chunks) == sample, "| longest:", max(map(len, chunks)))

    Output

    0  177 chars  'If you are a U.S. citi' ... ' on amount of refund. '
1  199 chars  'If you file your claim' ... ' before you filed the '
2  147 chars  'claim.\n\nTax paid. Paym' ... 'paid on the due date. '
3  136 chars  'For example, income ta' ... '15 for most taxpayers.'
lossless: True | longest: 199

    No chunk is over 200 characters, and the chunks join back to the exact input because split_keep keeps every separator. Greedy packing shows too: chunk 0 took the next paragraph's heading, and chunk 2 starts with "claim.".

    Measured: splitters and sizes on IRS Publication 17

    A sample shows the mechanics, not which splitter retrieves better, so we measured that on IRS Publication 17 (2025), the IRS's 142-page guide to individual income tax. It is a US government work ("All material in this publication may be reprinted freely"), and we used it before in a post about retrieval and fine-tuning. This block downloads the 2025 edition from the IRS archive, extracts the text with pypdf and removes the proof-sheet lines (running footers stay in):

    import hashlib, os, urllib.request
from pypdf import PdfReader

if not os.path.exists("p17.pdf"):
    urllib.request.urlretrieve("https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-prior/p17--2025.pdf", "p17.pdf")
print("sha256:", hashlib.sha256(open("p17.pdf", "rb").read()).hexdigest()[:16])

raw = [page.extract_text() for page in PdfReader("p17.pdf").pages]
print("pages:", len(raw), "| blank-line paragraphs:", sum(len(re.split(r"\n\s*\n", p)) for p in raw))

FURNITURE = re.compile(r"^(Userid:|AH XSL/XML Fileid|Page \d+ of \d+|The type and rule above).*$", re.M)

def clean(page):
    page = FURNITURE.sub("", page)                  # proof-sheet lines on every page
    page = re.sub(r"-[ \t]*\n(?=[a-z])", "", page)  # re-join words hyphenated at line ends
    page = re.sub(r"\s*\n\s*", " ", page)           # PDF line breaks become spaces
    return re.sub(r" {2,}", " ", page).strip()

text = "\n\n".join(clean(p) for p in raw)           # keep page breaks as the only "\n\n"
print("characters:", len(text))

    Output

    sha256: 2d2381d62c7c77ed
pages: 142 | blank-line paragraphs: 157
characters: 952548

    pypdf returned 157 blank-line blocks for 142 pages: the paragraphs did not survive extraction, so a paragraph splitter here would be a page splitter, and in the recursive splitter ". " does most of the work. The hash shows the edition.

    Questions. 30 single-fact questions, drafted with an AI assistant while reading the publication and fixed before any retrieval ran (one was reworded for clarity after review), phrased the way a person asks ("A friend did my taxes for free. Should they sign the return as the preparer?"). Each is paired with a short verbatim span of the text that answers it, and a retrieved chunk counts only if it contains the whole span. They are published as pub17-chunking-questions.json.

    Setup. Each splitter from above on the full text: recursive at 256, 512 and 1,024 characters, and fixed-size, fixed-size with 40 characters of overlap and sentence packing at 256. Embeddings from all-MiniLM-L6-v2 at a pinned revision, cosine similarity, and BM25 from rank-bm25 as a keyword baseline. The budget column takes 2,048 ÷ size chunks (8, 4 or 2) and prints the characters they actually hold.

    import json, math, time
import numpy as np
from sentence_transformers import SentenceTransformer
from rank_bm25 import BM25Okapi

qs = json.load(open("pub17-chunking-questions.json"))["questions"]
model = SentenceTransformer("sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2", device="cpu",
                            revision="1110a243fdf4706b3f48f1d95db1a4f5529b4d41")
q_vecs = model.encode([q["question"] for q in qs], normalize_embeddings=True)
words = lambda s: re.findall(r"[a-z0-9]+", s.lower())
evidence = [[m.span() for m in re.finditer(re.escape(q["evidence"]), text)] for q in qs]

def spans_of(chunks, overlap=0):
    starts = np.cumsum([0] + [len(c) - overlap for c in chunks[:-1]])
    return [(s, s + len(c)) for s, c in zip(starts, chunks)]

def found(ranked, spans, k):
    # relevant = the chunk contains a whole occurrence of the evidence span
    return [any(a <= s and e <= b for j in ranked[i][:k] for a, b in [spans[j]] for s, e in evidence[i])
            for i in range(len(qs))]

def mcnemar(x, y):
    b = sum(p and not q for p, q in zip(x, y))
    c = sum(q and not p for p, q in zip(x, y))
    p = min(1.0, 2 * sum(math.comb(b + c, i) for i in range(min(b, c) + 1)) / 2 ** (b + c))
    return f"{b} vs {c} questions only one found, exact McNemar p = {p:.3f}"

setups = [("recursive", 256, lambda: (recursive_split(text, 256), 0)),
          ("recursive", 512, lambda: (recursive_split(text, 512), 0)),
          ("recursive", 1024, lambda: (recursive_split(text, 1024), 0)),
          ("fixed", 256, lambda: (fixed_size(text, 256), 0)),
          ("fixed+40", 256, lambda: (fixed_size(text, 256, overlap=40), 40)),
          ("sentence", 256, lambda: (pack(sentences(text), 256), 0))]
hits, top = {}, {}
print("splitter   size  chunks  >256tok  dense@5  dense@budget (k, chars)  bm25@5")
for name, size, make in setups:
    t0 = time.time()
    chunks, overlap = make()
    spans = spans_of(chunks, overlap)
    assert all(text[a:b] == c for (a, b), c in zip(spans, chunks))
    vecs = model.encode([c.strip() for c in chunks], normalize_embeddings=True, batch_size=64)
    dense = np.argsort(-(q_vecs @ vecs.T), axis=1)
    bm25 = BM25Okapi([words(c) for c in chunks])
    lexical = [np.argsort(-bm25.get_scores(words(q["question"]))) for q in qs]
    too_long = sum(len(model.tokenizer(c.strip())["input_ids"]) > model.max_seq_length for c in chunks)
    k = 2048 // size
    sent = np.mean([sum(len(chunks[j]) for j in dense[i][:k]) for i in range(len(qs))])
    hits[name, size] = found(dense, spans, k), found(lexical, spans, 5)
    top[name, size] = [chunks[dense[i][0]] for i in range(len(qs))]
    print(f"{name:<9} {size:>5}  {len(chunks):>6}  {too_long:>7}  {sum(found(dense, spans, 5)):>4}/30  "
          f"{sum(hits[name, size][0]):>8}/30 ({k}, {sent:,.0f})  {sum(hits[name, size][1]):>8}/30  "
          f"{time.time() - t0:.0f} s")

print("dense, same budget, recursive 256 vs 1024:", mcnemar(hits["recursive", 256][0], hits["recursive", 1024][0]))
print("bm25 top 5, recursive 512 vs 256:", mcnemar(hits["recursive", 512][1], hits["recursive", 256][1]))
i = next(i for i, (a, b) in enumerate(zip(hits["recursive", 256][0], hits["recursive", 1024][0])) if a and not b)
print("found only with 256:", qs[i]["question"])
print("  1024 top hit starts:", repr(" ".join(top["recursive", 1024][i].split())[:70]))

    • At top 5, nothing separates. All six setups land between 19 and 23 of 30.
    • At a similar budget, small recursive chunks lead. 256-character chunks held the answer for 25 questions while returning 1,604 characters on average; 1,024-character chunks held it for 18 while returning 1,812. Nine questions were found only by the small chunks and two only by the large (exact McNemar test, p = 0.065): suggestive, not significant.
    • Boundaries looked more useful than overlap. On these 30 questions (not tested for significance), at 256 characters, recursive and sentence packing both reached 25; plain fixed-size reached 21, and 40 characters of overlap did not change that. Sentence packing also left 23 chunks over the model's limit, because the tax tables and the index have no sentence ends.
    • BM25 went the other way (19 of 30 at 512 against 14 at 256, p = 0.125), which on 30 questions is not significant either.
    • Long chunks were truncated. 138 of 1,057 chunks at 1,024 characters pass the 256-word-piece limit; 86 of them are on the tax table, worksheet and rate-schedule pages (PDF pages 113 to 127), where numbers split into many word pieces. At 512, all 89 are on tax-table pages (114 to 124).

    Limits: one document, one small model trained on short inputs, 30 single-fact questions (which favour small chunks), and retrieval only, not answers. A first run counted a chunk holding half the span and gave 27 against 18 (p = 0.012); we switched to the whole span after review, since half a span can miss the number that answers the question.

    Variations and parameters

    ChoiceWhat it changesDefault on this page
    Chunk sizeSmall chunks match precisely and cost little context per hit; large ones bring more surrounding text. Stay inside the model's input limit.200; 256 to 1,024 measured
    OverlapKeeps text near a cut whole in at least one chunk, at the cost of more chunks and duplicate hits.0; 40 in one sample
    Unit: characters or tokensTokens match the model's limit exactly, which matters for numbers, code and non-English text.Characters
    BoundaryNone (fixed-size), sentence, paragraph or recursive: whether chunks start and end where the text does.Recursive: "\n\n", "\n", ". ", " ", then characters
    PackingGreedy packing fills each chunk and can glue a heading to the paragraph before it; one unit per chunk leaves sizes uneven.Greedy
    Top kHigher k raises recall and the context you send. Compare sizes at equal k × size.5, and 2,048 ÷ size

    Pitfalls

    • Chunks over the model's limit are cut quietly. The all-MiniLM-L6-v2 model card says input over 256 word pieces is truncated, and that training used at most 128 tokens. Above, 138 of 1,057 chunks at 1,024 characters lost their ends. Count tokens with the model's tokenizer.
    • Fixed-size cuts start chunks mid-word. When BrewSLM's cleaning step cut by character count, 403 of 467 passages of a legal corpus began mid-word, which is why it now cuts on sentence boundaries. The numbers are in the docstring of chunk_text in cleaning_service.py.
    • PDF text loses paragraphs and gains page furniture. pypdf gave 157 blank-line blocks for 142 pages (above), and 173 of 1,103 first-pass Pub 17 passages carried a repeated page header or footer (IRS Pub 17 post).
    • Comparing chunk sizes at a fixed k hides the cost. At top 5 the 1,024-character run tied with the rest while sending about four times the text (output above). Compare at equal k × size.

    In BrewSLM: Data → Prepare packs whole sentences into passages of at most 1,000 characters by default (up to 100 characters of whole sentences repeat in the next), longer than suggested here; check it with the documents → Q&A retrieval sweep.

    Practice

    1. With 1,024-character chunks and all-MiniLM-L6-v2, dense search can't match facts near the end of long chunks. Why, and what are two fixes?

    The model truncates input at 256 word pieces, so the end of a long chunk is never embedded. Fixes: smaller chunks (256-character chunks never hit the limit above), or splitting by the model's own token count; a model with a longer input limit also works, but measure again.

    2. Setup A finds the answer in the top 5 for 23 of 30 questions with 1,024-character chunks; setup B finds 22 with 256-character chunks. Is A better?

    Not from those numbers. One question in 30 is noise, and A sends four times as much text at k = 5. Compare at equal k × size on the same questions, then run a paired test such as McNemar's on the questions only one setup found.

    3. Write a check that a splitter with overlap lost no text.

    Drop the repeated prefix from every chunk after the first and join: for fixed_size(text, size, overlap), chunks[0] + "".join(c[overlap:] for c in chunks[1:]) == text. Without overlap it is "".join(chunks) == text, which the recursive splitter passes because it keeps every separator.
