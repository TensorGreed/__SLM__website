RAG: retrieval-augmented generation

RAG answers a question by first retrieving passages from your documents and then giving them to a model with the question. Most of the quality is decided before the model sees anything: how the documents were cut, how passages are scored, and how many are sent. These pages take one of those decisions at a time, with code you can run on a CPU.

One corpus, measured throughout

Every page measures on the same material, so numbers can be compared across pages: IRS Publication 17 (2025), cut into 2,170 chunks of up to 512 characters, and 80 questions frozen before any run, each answered by a verbatim span of the publication (30 + 50, with short answer keys for 47). Differences are tested on the same questions, and called suggestive when the test can't separate them. Generation pages use a 1.5B open model on CPU, so their answer-quality numbers are small-model numbers.

For a full project rather than one topic, read how retrieval beat a fine-tune on the same tax questions in the IRS Publication 17 post, or see what BrewSLM checks before it trains in Documents → Q&A. Embeddings have their own track.
