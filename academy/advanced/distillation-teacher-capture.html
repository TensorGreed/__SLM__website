    Knowledge distillation I: the teacher and capturing its logits

    After this lesson you can explain why a teacher's soft probabilities carry more signal than hard labels, why BrewSLM captures distillation offline as the teacher's answer plus top-k logprobs, and why teacher and student tokenizers should match (and what happens when they don't).

    Level: advanced Read time: ~11 min Prerequisites: Beyond a single SFT run: the advanced toolkit Updated: 13 Nov 2026

    You can train a 135M model to do a task. But what if a 7B model does it noticeably better, and you still need the small one's speed and cost? Knowledge distillation closes that gap: the big teacher trains the small student to imitate it, transferring more than a label file ever could.

    Why mimic, instead of just using more labels?

    A hard label says "this review is positive" — one bit. The teacher's full output is a probability distribution over the vocabulary: "92% positive, 7% neutral, 1% negative." That distribution encodes the teacher's uncertainty and its sense of how classes relate — the "dark knowledge" a one-hot label throws away. Training the student to match the distribution (its soft targets) transfers far more signal per example than the label alone.

    From Track 1

    Recall that the model's final layer produces logits, and softmax turns them into probabilities. Distillation supervises the student on the teacher's softmaxed logits, not just the gold token — the same machinery, a richer target.

    Offline distillation: run the teacher once

    The teacher is big and slow. Running it on every training step (online distillation) is expensive. BrewSLM does offline distillation: run the teacher across your dataset once, save what it produced, then train the student against that saved file as many times as you like. The capture step runs as an in-memory background task (not a row in the jobs table, so it doesn't show in the notification bell); you poll it for progress:

    POST /api/projects/<id>/distillation/capture     → starts the capture task (202)
GET  /api/projects/<id>/distillation/tasks/<task_id>  → its progress + result
# writes one row per example to:
#   data/projects/<id>/distillation/teacher_capture.jsonl

    Capture top-k logprobs, not the whole vocabulary

    Saving the teacher's full distribution would mean a probability for every vocabulary token, per position — and vocabularies run from tens to hundreds of thousands of tokens (SmolLM2's has 49,152). Almost all of that mass sits on a handful of tokens, so the capture keeps only the top-k per position: the k most likely tokens and their log-probabilities.

    BrewSLM calls the teacher through a chat-completions-style endpoint with logprobs on, at temperature 0 and up to 256 new tokens. k defaults to 10 and is capped at 20, because that API caps top_logprobs at 20. Each captured row keeps your original fields and adds the teacher's own answer plus, for every token of that answer, the token string and its top-k alternatives:

    { "prompt": "Review: arrived broken, refund took a month. Sentiment?",   # your row
  "teacher_completion": "negative",                  # the teacher's own answer
  "teacher_logits": [                                # one entry per answer token
    { "token": "negative",
      "top_k": [["negative", -0.08], ["neutral", -2.9], ["positive", -4.1]] }
  ],
  "teacher_model": "...", "source": "teacher_capture" }

    The three alternatives here are the sentiment words because, at the answer position, the teacher's vocabulary distribution concentrates on them. Top-k keeps the meaningful part of the distribution at a fraction of the size. What it drops is the tail: at train time the missing tokens get a large negative logit (≈ zero probability), which is fine for peaked positions but loses information where the teacher is genuinely unsure.

    Because the student trains on prompt + teacher_completion, it learns the teacher's answer (sequence-level distillation) as well as its per-token distribution over that answer.

    Tokenizers: matched is best, mismatched is weaker

    Per-token soft targets line up exactly only when teacher and student share a tokenizer. BrewSLM stores token strings, so at train time it maps each one onto the student's vocabulary and drops the ones the student lacks; the mapped and dropped counts (the map rate) appear in the run's warnings as Offline KD record stats. A low map rate means a weak soft signal, not gibberish, and the student still learns the teacher's answers. For the strongest signal, pick a teacher and student from the same family (e.g. a larger and a smaller model of one series). With a different tokenizer, plain sequence-level distillation — training on the teacher's generated answers (Kim & Rush, 2016) — needs no shared vocabulary at all.

    With the teacher's knowledge frozen into teacher_capture.jsonl, the student never needs the teacher again. The next lesson is the loss that actually transfers that knowledge — the KD objective.

    On the platform

    A BrewSLM run reaches offline KD two ways: in the Train form, More options → Training presets → apply an Offline KD preset (recipe.kd.*; it sets training_mode: distillation, distillation_offline: true, alpha and temperature), or through the experiment API: POST /api/projects/{id}/training/experiments accepts training_mode: distillation (or distillation_offline: true) plus distillation_capture_path and distillation_offline_top_k (defaults distillation/teacher_capture.jsonl and 8). The capture file must sit inside the project's own data folder (by default its distillation/teacher_capture.jsonl), distillation_offline_top_k is at most 20, and offline KD together with live-teacher KD (distillation_enabled) is refused. The Training Mode list itself offers only SFT, continued pretraining, DPO and ORPO, so after applying an Offline KD preset it can't display the mode it will send (Lesson 3.11).

    Key idea

    Distillation trains a small student on the teacher's soft targets — the full probability distribution, which carries 'dark knowledge' hard labels lack. BrewSLM captures it offline — the teacher's answer plus top-k logprobs per token — so the costly teacher runs once. A shared tokenizer gives the cleanest soft targets; a mismatch lowers the map rate and weakens them.

    Key terms

    knowledge distillation
    Training a small student model to reproduce a larger teacher model's outputs.
    teacher / student
    The large source model and the small model being trained to mimic it.
    soft targets
    The teacher's full probability distribution over tokens — a richer signal than a hard label.
    dark knowledge
    The relational information in a soft distribution (which wrong answers are plausible) that one-hot labels discard.
    top-k logprobs
    The k most likely tokens and their log-probabilities per position (BrewSLM: k = 10 by default, at most 20); a compact capture of the teacher's distribution.
    offline distillation
    Running the teacher once to a capture file, then training the student against it repeatedly.
    map rate
    The share of the teacher's captured token strings that exist in the student's vocabulary; unmapped tokens are dropped, so a low rate weakens the soft signal.
    sequence-level distillation
    Training the student on the teacher's generated answers; works across tokenizers.
